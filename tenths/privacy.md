# Raincard privacy policy

Last updated November 16, 2026

Raincard doesn't collect any data. It has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and it doesn't send anything to us.

## Rain numbers from NOAA

To show how much rain fell at a place, Raincard sends that place's map coordinates to three public services:

- the National Weather Service's map services at mapservices.weather.noaa.gov, for the radar estimates and the first estimate of the newest day,
- the National Weather Service at api.weather.gov, for the forecast,
- the Applied Climate Information System at data.rcc-acis.org, which NOAA's Regional Climate Centers run, for the daily record, the gauges nearby and the normals.

Raincard asks them when you add a place or move its pin, when you open Raincard and a place's numbers are over an hour old, when you pull down to refresh, and when iOS lets it refresh in the background. Opening a day sends a box around the place to look for gauges nearby. The map sends the area on screen to draw the rain, and a spot you tap to get its total.

Nothing about you or your gauge readings goes with these requests, though like any website these services see your iPhone's internet address. They're run by or for NOAA, a US government agency, and what the Weather Service keeps from these requests is covered by the [NWS privacy policy](https://www.weather.gov/privacy).

## Your location

If you tap Use my location when adding a place, Raincard asks for your location once and uses it as the place's spot. That spot is saved as the place, stored on your iPhone and sent to NOAA as above. Raincard doesn't follow where you go. You can also add a place by searching an address or dropping a pin, without location access.

## The map and search

Address search, the names of dropped pins and the maps use Apple Maps, which Apple's privacy policy covers.

## What stays on your iPhone

Your places, their rain record, your gauge readings, weekly targets and settings are all stored on your iPhone. Raincard's widgets read them there. The morning note is written and shown by your iPhone, with no server in between.

## Exports

When you share a year as a PDF or CSV, the file goes only where you send it.

## Purchases

Raincard Unlimited is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the payment, and Raincard only learns whether Unlimited is active.

## Keeping and deleting your data

Everything Raincard stores stays on your iPhone until you delete it. To remove a gauge reading, tap its day, then Remove reading. To delete a place with its rain record and your readings, press and hold it in Places and tap Delete place, or tap Edit on its page, then Delete place. Deleting the app removes everything Raincard stored. If you back up your iPhone, the backup includes Raincard's data, as it does for your other apps. Files you shared stay wherever you sent them. To take back location access or turn off the morning note's notifications, go to Settings, then Apps, then Raincard.

## Children

Raincard collects nothing from anyone, children included.
