# Woodblock privacy policy

Last updated November 15, 2026

Woodblock doesn't collect any data. It has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and it doesn't send anything to us. It doesn't use the camera or your location. It uses the microphone only while listening is on, as described below.

## What Woodblock keeps

Your metronome setup, sounds, volumes and settings are stored on your device. So is the delay Headphone sync measured for each pair of headphones you synced.

With Woodblock Practice, your songs and setlists are stored on your device too. The practice log keeps when the click played, for how long, the song or lesson piece that was loaded, the tempos it played at and whether a speed trainer reached its target. It records from the first launch, bought or not, so your history is already there when you unlock it.

## The microphone

Listening, part of Woodblock Practice, shows where your notes land against the beat. Woodblock asks for the microphone the first time you turn listening on, never before, and the free metronome never uses it.

While the click plays with listening on, Woodblock hears your instrument through the microphone and works out, in memory on your device, when each note started. The sound itself isn't recorded, saved or sent anywhere, and the microphone turns off when the click stops. The practice log keeps two numbers from each session: how far ahead of or behind the beat your notes landed on average, and how closely they sat together.

## Lessons

A lesson is a link. Everything in it, the pieces with their tempos and notes and the names typed for who it's from and who it's for, is written into the link after the # sign. Browsers don't send that part of a link to the website, so when a lesson opens on its web page, the page reads it on your device and the lesson never reaches us. GitHub, which hosts the page, receives what any browser sends when it loads a page, such as your IP address, but not the lesson.

A lesson you open in Woodblock is kept on your device until you delete it, and the minutes you play its pieces go into your practice log. Send the week makes a picture and a few lines of text from those minutes, and they go only where you send them from the share sheet. If you send lessons, Woodblock keeps the name you type under From on your device, so you don't type it each time.

## iCloud

If iCloud is on for Woodblock, it keeps a copy of your songs, setlists, lessons and practice log in your own iCloud account, so they match on your iPhone, iPad and Mac. Apple stores that copy for you, and we can't see it. With iCloud off, everything stays on your device.

## Siri, widgets and the Lock Screen

Woodblock gives Siri the names of your songs, so that "Play Amazing Grace in Woodblock" finds the right one. Apple handles what you say to Siri, as it does for every app. The widgets, the Lock Screen and the Control Center control read the tempo, whether the click is playing and your practice streak from Woodblock on your device.

## Purchases

Woodblock Practice is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the payment, and Woodblock only learns whether your purchase is active.

## Keeping and deleting your data

Everything stays until you delete it. Deleting a song, setlist or lesson in Woodblock also deletes it from your other devices through iCloud. Deleting the app removes what Woodblock stored on your device, and the copy in iCloud stays until you delete it: in the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, open your iCloud storage and choose Woodblock. If you back up your device, the backup includes Woodblock's data, as it does for your other apps.

To stop syncing, turn Woodblock off in the list of apps that use iCloud, under your name and iCloud in the Settings app. To take back the microphone, turn Woodblock off under Privacy & Security, then Microphone, in the Settings app. The click keeps playing without listening.

## Children

Woodblock collects nothing from anyone, children included. A lesson a teacher sends goes from their device to the student's in the link, and nothing in it passes through us.
